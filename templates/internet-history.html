Introduction

The Internet developed over many years through the work of researchers, universities, governments, and technology organizations. It started with early research on how computers could communicate and share information, which later led to the creation of ARPANET and the development of TCP/IP. These technologies allowed different networks to connect with each other and then became the foundation of the modern Internet. Later, the World Wide Web made the Internet much easier for people around the world to access and use.

Events and Timeline

Packet Switching (1960s)

Researchers developed packet-switching technology, which divides data into small packets that can travel through a network. Packet switching became one of the fundamental technologies behind modern computer networks.

Source: https://www.computerhistory.org/internethistory/1960s/

ARPANET (1969)

ARPANET connected its first four computers at UCLA, Stanford Research Institute, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah. ARPANET demonstrated that computers could communicate over a packet-switched network and became an important predecessor of the Internet.

Source: https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/arpanet

TCP/IP Developed (1970s)

Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the concepts that became TCP/IP. TCP/IP allowed different networks to communicate with one another, creating the foundation for an interconnected "network of networks."

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet-related-networks/

TCP/IP Becomes the Standard (1983)

ARPANET officially switched from NCP to TCP/IP on January 1, 1983. This established TCP/IP as the main communication protocol for the growing Internet.

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

DNS Introduced (1983)

The Domain Name System (DNS) was developed to translate human-readable domain names into numerical IP addresses. DNS made the growing Internet easier to use because people could use names instead of remembering numerical addresses.

Source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc882/

World Wide Web Proposed (1989)

Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN. The Web provided an easy way to access and connect information using hypertext. It later became one of the main ways people used the Internet.

Source: https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/

Key Technologies

Packet Switching

It is a method of dividing data into small packets that can travel through a network independently. It became important when it was developed during the 1960s and used as a foundation for ARPANET. It became so important because It allowed computer networks to use communication resources efficiently and became a fundamental part of Internet networking.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a foundational collection of networking protocols developed during the 1970s to enable communication between different computers and systems. Its adoption by ARPANET in 1983 marked a major turning point, as it established a unified standard for data transfer. By allowing distinct and previously incompatible networks to connect seamlessly with one another, TCP/IP laid the essential framework for what eventually became the modern Internet.

DNS

Introduced in 1983, the Domain Name System (DNS) is a core network protocol that translates human-readable domain names into numerical IP addresses. Before its creation, users had to memorize complex strings of numbers to reach specific servers. By allowing people to navigate using simple, memorable names instead, DNS made the Internet significantly more intuitive and user-friendly.
